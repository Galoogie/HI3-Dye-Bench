01

How the game mixes dye

Picture a standard color wheel. Every ingredient is a push in one direction on that wheel. Raspberry pushes toward red, Blueberry toward blue, Kelp toward green.

Two things decide how hard an ingredient pushes: how strong it is, and how many you put in. Raspberry is strong and pushes 10 per unit; Dandelion is faint and pushes 5 per unit. Ten Raspberry pushes with a force of 100, while ten Dandelion only push with a force of 50.

So you mix two ingredients together, lets say five Lotus Flower and five Blueberry. Neither add darkening, so you should get a nice, vivid green! But you don’t. You get an ugly, muddy grey. Why?

What makes predicting a mix of ingredients difficult is how opposites react to each other. A push that cancels out doesn't just vanish: it comes off the total brightness. Mix two strong colors from opposite sides of the wheel and most of their force is spent fighting each other, so what you get is not a bright color in between. It is mud. The harder they fight, the darker it gets. In the example of the Lotus Flower and Blueberry, they are almost exactly opposite of each other on the color wheel, producing a muddy grey.

Finally, some ingredients darken on their own, no matter what else is in the pot. Acorn, Coal, Blackberry and Black Nightshade are the obvious ones, but plenty of colored ingredients darken too. Enough of any of them turns the dye black.

04

The exact formulas

Everything above comes from these five steps. They are written out in full so you can check the arithmetic by hand, or build your own tool from them.

Constants
ingredient i has hue Hᵢsaturation per unit sᵢdarkening per unit dᵢ
Three fixed numbers per ingredient and nothing else. They are listed in the table below. nᵢ is how many of that ingredient you put in.
1 · Weights
wᵢ = sᵢ × nᵢ
How hard this ingredient pushes: its strength times how many you used.
2 · Vector sum
x = Σ wᵢ · cos(Hᵢ)
y = Σ wᵢ · sin(Hᵢ)
Each ingredient becomes an arrow of length wᵢ pointing at its own hue. Add the arrows.
3 · Hue
H = atan2(y, x)
→ degrees, add 360 if negative
The direction the combined arrow points. Darkening never touches this.
4 · Saturation
S = min(100, √(x² + y²))
The length of the combined arrow, capped at 100.
5 · Value
V = base − (Σ wᵢ − √(x² + y²)) − Σ dᵢnᵢ
clamped to 0–100
base = 100, or 99 when Σ wᵢ = 0
Start at 100. Subtract the push that cancelled out; everything you added minus what survived. Then subtract every ingredient's darkening. The base drops to 99 when nothing in the pot has a hue, which is why pure greys land one step darker than the arithmetic suggests.
